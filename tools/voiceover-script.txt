ELEVENLABS SCRIPT — interactive routine voice over

RECORD EACH LINE AS ITS OWN GENERATION. Eight short generations, eight
downloads, named as below. Do not try to put them in one file.

Why: a single-file take was tried twice and could not be split safely. The
second attempt used <break time="3.0s" /> separators, which ElevenLabs simply
did not honour -- the longest gap in the returned audio was 0.68s, against
in-sentence pauses of 0.30-0.62s. Nothing distinguishes a line break from a
breath, and a mis-assigned line means the wrong words at the most exposed
moment of the routine. Separate generations remove the problem rather than
managing it.

VOICE SETTINGS
Calm, warm, unhurried. Slightly slower than conversational. These are
instructions spoken to a room over a PA while people search a screen, not
narration read to someone holding a phone. No rising inflection at the ends --
they are instructions, not questions. Keep the level even across all eight.

THE EIGHT LINES
Paste each block on its own, generate, download, and name the file as shown.

--- intro.mp3 ---
Look at the screen. <break time="0.4s" /> Take a moment. <break time="0.4s" /> And think of any one of these.

--- round1.mp3 ---
Now, <break time="0.4s" /> move to the closest one that has a face. <break time="0.4s" /> Not your own. <break time="0.4s" /> If two look equally close, take either.

--- round2.mp3 ---
Now, <break time="0.4s" /> move to the food nearest to you.

--- round3.mp3 ---
Now, <break time="0.4s" /> move to the nearest object you could pick up and hold. <break time="0.4s" /> Not food, <break time="0.4s" /> and nothing alive.

--- round4.mp3 ---
Now, <break time="0.4s" /> move to the animal nearest to you.

--- round5-logo.mp3 ---
And finally. <break time="0.4s" /> Move to the nearest logo.

--- round5-green.mp3 ---
And finally. <break time="0.4s" /> Move to the green thing nearest to you.

--- hold.mp3 ---
Now stay exactly where you are. <break time="0.4s" /> Don't move. <break time="0.4s" /> Lock it in, <break time="0.4s" /> and keep your eyes on it.

NOTE ON ROUND 3
"Not food, and nothing alive" is not padding. Without the first, every carrot
and burger on screen answers the instruction; without the second, so do the
animals. Both exclusions are load-bearing -- the routine failed in performance
because that line was ambiguous.

IF YOU DO END UP WITH ONE FILE ANYWAY
tools/split-voiceover.sh will try, but it refuses to write anything unless it
finds exactly 8 segments separated by gaps over 1.5s. Trust that refusal.
